# Numerical Method Comparison — Evidence for the Choice of the Reference Solver

**Project:** SMVac — Naman Goyal (namangoyal31), IISc Bangalore
**Plan:** docs/STAGE_C_PLAN.md §6.
**Raw data:**

* `validation/results/method_comparison.csv`, produced by `validation/method_comparison.py`;
* `validation/results/convergence.csv`, produced by `validation/convergence_study.py`.

The tables below are generated by `validation/summarize_comparison.py`.

All methods solve the same problem with the same representation of $\lambda(x)$, so only the method differs.
The **consensus** value of a case is the median of the successful runs of M1, M2 and M4. These are three
structurally different methods: two shooting formulations and global spectral collocation.

## 1. Candidates

| | Method | Formulation |
|---|---|---|
| M0 | legacy construction (FL profile, first-order action, minimised over $R$) | not a solution of the bounce equation; included for traceability |
| M1 | overshoot/undershoot shooting, adaptive DOP853, bisection on $\ln\phi(0)$ | physical variables $s=r\phi_0$, $u=\phi/\phi_0$; tail correction $u\simeq C/s^2$ |
| M2 | the same, in scale-covariant variables | $\tau=\ln r$, $f=r\phi$; hyperbolic end points $e^{\pm\tau}$ |
| M3 | global collocation BVP (`scipy.solve_bvp`, 4th order, adaptive mesh) | $(\tau,f)$, Robin end conditions, FL initial guess |
| M4 | Chebyshev spectral collocation + damped Newton, adaptive $N$ | $(\tau,f)$, Robin end conditions, FL initial guess |

**Benchmarks:**

* constant $\lambda$ (exact answer known);
* the parabolic model $\lambda=\lambda_0+\tfrac12\lambda_2(x-x_0)^2$ with $q=|\lambda_0|/\lambda_2$ from 250
  (thick wall) down to 0.1 (thin wall, nearly degenerate second vacuum);
* SM points in the controlled region;
* an approach to the absolute-stability boundary $M_t\to M_t^{\rm crit}=171.1193$ GeV at $M_h=125.15$ GeV;
* two exploratory points.

## 3. Findings

1. **M0 is unsound** even on perfectly smooth potentials.
   * Its error is $10^{-4}$ in the thick-wall case.
   * At $q=25$ it returns $-3\times10^{17}$, and at $q=2.5$ it is too large by a factor 4.7.
   * Near criticality it returns negative values ($-4.7\times10^6$, $-1.3\times10^{21}$, $-6.9\times10^{17}$),
     a 65% error, or a sentinel.

   This confirms the analysis in SCIENTIFIC_METHODOLOGY §6.3: the construction is unbounded below, and
   minimisation seeks out that regime.

2. **M1, M2 and M4 agree to $\lesssim10^{-11}$ relative wherever all three converge** (all parabolic models, all
   SM points), with one exception. At the closest near-critical point ($M_t=M_t^{\rm crit}+0.003$ GeV,
   $S\approx2\times10^6$) the spread is $2\times10^{-10}$. Identity residuals are $10^{-14}$–$10^{-10}$. Three
   formulations that share no discretisation agreeing this closely is one line of evidence that the reference is
   accurate. The other lines, which are independent of solver agreement, are in docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.md §4.

3. **Shooting needs a correct bracket.** The admissible starting values $\phi(0)$ lie between the barrier and the
   true vacuum, where $G<0$. This interval shrinks as the wall thins. With a naive scan, M1 and M2 failed at
   $q\le0.5$. Restricting the scan to that interval fixes it. M2 also needs two specific safeguards:
   * the undershoot test must be $\phi'>0$, i.e. $f'-f$ crossing zero upward, not $f'$ crossing zero;
   * trajectories that linger near the true vacuum, where $f$ grows exponentially, must be classified as
     overshoot.

   Without the first safeguard, the explicit integrator stalled on oscillations about the barrier top.

4. **M3 (low-order adaptive collocation) is the least robust.** It failed for every parabolic model with
   $q\le2.5$, for the two nearest near-critical SM points, and for the exploratory point (60, 150) GeV. In an
   earlier run, before its mesh was capped at 50,000 nodes, it exhausted a 500,000-node mesh on a near-critical
   point.

5. **M4 is the most accurate and cheapest in the thick-wall regime.** It converges exponentially in $N$
   (§4) and takes 0.1–0.5 s per SM point in Python. In C++ a full SM point (RG running, potential, analytic
   estimates, two resolutions) takes 0.36 s.

   Thin walls need more nodes: at $q=0.1$, fixed $N=256$ leaves identity residuals of $6\times10^{-7}$ and an
   action error of $8.6\times10^{-8}$ relative to the shooting consensus. The identity residual reliably detects
   this, so the resolution is doubled until both identities are below $10^{-9}$.

   When the bounce crosses the frozen-coupling kink at $M_{\rm Pl}$, convergence becomes algebraic. One
   such point is (127.1, 172.4), where $\phi(0)=10^{19.15}$ GeV. Its result is accepted in a flagged
   reduced-accuracy tier with a widened $\delta S$. There, M4 at $N=1536$ gives
   $12236.948\pm0.014$ and the independent M2 gives $12236.9494$.

6. **The bounce is not the dominant uncertainty of the decay exponent.** Near the metastability boundary
   ($450<S<650$), the FL error is 0.14–0.25 units of action. By contrast (`results/summary.json`):
   * the identification $\Lambda_B=\arg\min\lambda$ (literature) versus $\Lambda_B=1/R$ changes the threshold
     by 3.5–25 units (median 7.3);
   * the `cw1` and `tree` prescriptions differ by 2.6–5% in $S$ at 90% of points (median 2.7%).

7. **Near criticality and at the Planck kink** (added after the production scans; docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.md
   §5.2–5.3):
   * M4 with adaptive $N$ (up to 1024) and M2 agree to $\le1.1\times10^{-11}$ on the parabolic model down to
     $q=0.085$. Below that, only M4 converges, down to $q=0.05$.
   * On the SM lines, both fail for $q\lesssim0.05$ ($S\gtrsim5\times10^7$). These points are reported as
     unresolved.
   * Where the bounce crosses the Planck kink and M4 fails (360 `cw1` scan points), M1 and M2 agree to
     $\le5.3\times10^{-10}$ (78 points). At the other 282 points both find only undershoots: no finite bounce
     exists in the frozen-coupling potential.

## 4. Convergence

Full tables are in docs/VALIDATION_REPORT.md §4, and the figure is `figures/convergence.png`.

* **M4, thick and moderately thin walls.** The action is converged for $N\ge192$. Its remaining deviation from the
  shooting consensus (2–6e-12) is constant in $N$ and reflects the shooting reference's own quadrature floor.
  Re-solving the same discretisation in `long double` changes $S$ by $\le4\times10^{-15}$.
* **M4, thin wall ($q=0.1$).** Relative error 6e-5 at $N=256$, 9e-8 at 384, 8e-11 at 512, 8e-13 at 768. The
  adaptive identity test raises $N$ accordingly.
* **M2 and M1.** The error falls roughly in proportion to the integrator tolerance, reaching a plateau of
  (1–6)e-12 at rtol $\le10^{-10}$.
* **Potential representation.** Varying the Chebyshev degree of $\lambda(x)$ (128–512) and the RG tolerance
  ($10^{-10}$–$10^{-13}$) changes $S$ by $\le5\times10^{-11}$.

## 5. Decision

* **Primary numerical reference: M4 (Chebyshev spectral collocation)**, with adaptive resolution and identity-based
  acceptance, implemented in C++ (`src/bounce.cpp`).
* **Cross-checks:**
  * M1 and M2 in the independent Python implementation (`python/smvac/reference/bounce.py`);
  * the rigorous lower bound $S_{\rm FL}$ and the variational estimate $S_{\rm CGM}$ checked at every scan point;
  * C++ tests pinned to the multi-method consensus values (`tests/test_bounce.cpp`).
* **Not adopted:**
  * M3: insufficiently robust;
  * M0: unsound;
  * M1/M2 as primary: comparable accuracy, but 10–50× slower and dependent on the bracket logic.

Timings in the CSV are Python wall times measured while production scans ran in parallel. They are useful only
for relative comparison between methods, and even then imperfectly. The production cost was measured separately,
unloaded, with `build/smvac_point`:

* 0.36 s for a full SM point (RG running, potential, analytic estimates, two collocation resolutions);
* 2.3 s for a point that needs the reduced-accuracy tier (Planck kink, $N=768$).
